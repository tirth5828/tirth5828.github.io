## 1. Problem

The current site is visually interesting but unreadable at speed. Ten sections, four display typefaces, a matrix canvas, a typing effect, a custom cursor and a Harry Potter easter egg compete with the content. A visitor cannot answer *what does this person work on* in the first screen, which is the only job the first screen has.

Two concrete defects found while surveying it:

- A **withdrawn NeurIPS paper was still listed as "under review"** (withdrawn 2026-07-29). A false claim about his own record, live.
- A second stale NeurIPS reference in the research prose.

## 2. Audience and goal

**Primary audience: recruiters and hiring managers for ML roles.** Tirth is in an active search targeting a signed offer by 2026-12-02, and this site is its public surface.

Secondary: academic peers and workshop organisers, particularly around ECCV 2026 (Malmö, Sep 6–14). They are served by depth further down the page, not by the top of it.

**Success criterion:** a recruiter who lands cold can state what he works on after one screen, and can find proof without scrolling more than twice.

## 3. The through-line

Three of the strongest recent papers ask the same question in different domains:

| Paper | Venue | Underlying question |
|---|---|---|
| Compositional Non-Face Re-Identification Pressure | ECCV 2026 (main) | Identity survives **anonymisation** — it accumulates across releases |
| Unlearning Is Not Deletion | ECCV 2026 (U&ME) | Data survives **deletion** — recoverable from released artifacts |
| Prompt-Time Selective Semantic De-Identification | AAAI-27 (under review) | Data survives **redaction** — context re-identifies it |

**Removal is not absence.** The site is built as evidence for that claim.

⚠️ Accepted trade-off: this under-weights the graph/systems stream (ICNC routing, INFOCOM IoT planning, topology + gradient descent). Those remain discoverable in the Research section. The judgement is that a site with a point of view beats a site that lists everything, and the CV can flex per application in a way a website cannot.

## 4. Information architecture

Ten sections collapse to five.

1. **Hero** — thesis sentence, flat credential line, two exits (selected work, email).
2. **Selected work** — the three papers above. Title, venue chip, one sentence on what each found. Preceded by a one-line statement of the through-line.

### Selected work copy (DRAFT — unverified, see §8.1)

Lead-in: **Three different systems. The same answer each time: removing information is not the same as it being gone.**

1. **Compositional Non-Face Re-Identification Pressure under Cumulative Vision Releases** · `ECCV 2026`
   You can be re-identified from vision data containing no faces at all, because ordinary non-face cues compose. And the risk accumulates across releases rather than resetting with each one.
2. **Unlearning Is Not Deletion: Auditing Residual Information in Released Vision Artifacts** · `ECCV 2026 · U&ME`
   Machine unlearning is judged by how a model behaves. But the artifacts we actually ship carry more than behaviour, so a model can pass every forgetting test and still hand over what it forgot.
3. **Prompt-Time Selective Semantic De-Identification for Medical LLMs** · `AAAI-27 · under review`
   Redacting a clinical note isn't enough when the surrounding context re-identifies the patient. The de-identification has to happen at prompt time and against the actual threat, not against a fixed template.

4. **Research** — full publication list as compact rows, grouped *accepted / under review / in preparation*, with a mono venue column aligned down the page. Followed by a short strip: topology work with Prof. Gidea, ELLIS Munich 2026 (selected), RLSS 2025 (selected), and one line linking the quantum-ML Medium series.
5. **Contact** — availability, email, three links. No form.

### ⚠️ Revision 2026-08-09 — experience moved above research

Shipped order is **hero → selected work → experience → research → contact**, not the research-first order originally specified.

**Why.** Tirth flagged that the site read as a research profile while he is also applying for industry ML roles, and that experience sat fourth of five. That was a real defect: a hiring manager scanning for *has this person shipped anything* had to clear a thesis, three papers and a full publication list before finding out he built a production system at A&A.

**What was rejected, and why.** He proposed a stats band after the hero (total accepted papers, years of experience). Declined for three reasons: the old site already had exactly that (`5 papers · 4 advisors · IEEE ICNC · ACM · NeurIPS · ECCV`) and removing it was the point of the redesign; stat grids are the most templated component in the genre; and **"5 accepted" is a weaker claim than "two papers at ECCV 2026 as a master's student in one year"** — a counter invites the wrong comparison.

A two-column Research/Engineering split after the hero was built and tested as variant B. **Rejected on inspection:** it repeated *"Two papers at ECCV 2026"* four lines below where the standfirst already said it, and it split the eye immediately under a headline that works because it has one focal point.

**Adopted instead:** reorder, plus rewrite the standfirst so it carries the engineering signal in prose. This puts the evidence higher than the split managed, at zero structural cost. Final standfirst:

> MS in Artificial Intelligence at Yeshiva University. **Two papers at ECCV 2026**, Best Poster at NSIA. Currently building production ML at **A&A Coatings**. Previously cross-chain routing at DZap and LLM infrastructure at ComputeLib.

⭐ Naming DZap and ComputeLib is deliberate. *"ML engineering across DeFi and IoT"* is a category; proper nouns are places that hired him, and the eye stops on them.

## 5. Visual system

**Type** — four display families reduced to two voices:

| Role | Face |
|---|---|
| Headline | Newsreader (serif) |
| Body / UI | IBM Plex Sans |
| Data, venue chips, dates | IBM Plex Mono |

Serif headline against a technical sans is the entire personality budget, spent once so nothing else has to be loud.

**Colour** — near-monochrome, single accent. Deliberately avoids warm-cream-and-terracotta, near-black-with-acid-green, and blue gradients.

```
LIGHT                          DARK
ground   #FBFBFA               ground   #0E1112
surface  #FFFFFF               surface  #16191A
ink      #16181C               ink      #E9EBEA
muted    #6E7278               muted    #8B9295
rule     #E4E5E2               rule     #262B2C
accent   #0F5257               accent   #5FB3B8
```

Accent appears only in: links, venue chips for accepted work, section eyebrows, focus rings.

**Layout** — single column, left-aligned throughout, nothing centred. Prose capped ~68ch; publication rows wider with an aligned mono venue column so venues scan vertically. Sections separated by space, not boxes.

**Motion** — one quiet scroll reveal, honouring `prefers-reduced-motion`. Nothing else.

## 6. Technical approach

- Same three files rewritten: `index.html`, `style.css`, `main.js`. No framework, no build step — Tirth must be able to edit it unaided.
- JS limited to theme toggle + scroll reveal. Target under 2KB from ~12KB.
- Both themes designed; `prefers-color-scheme` with a manual toggle persisted to `localStorage`.
- Accessibility is a requirement: semantic landmarks, correct heading order, visible focus rings, ≥4.5:1 contrast in both themes, skip link, honest alt text.
- Meta: title, description, Open Graph card. The site currently previews as nothing when shared.
- Weight target: roughly half current (47KB HTML / 34KB CSS / 12KB JS).
