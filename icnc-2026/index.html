Hierarchical Graph Representation for Multi-Chain Blockchain Routing

Tirth Joshi, Honggang Wang

IEEE ICNC 2026, Invited Talks track · Maui, February 2026

Paper (DOI) YU News

Assets on blockchains are built out of other assets. A liquidity-pool share needs both of its underlying tokens; a vault receipt needs the pool share. A flat graph of tokens cannot say “needs all of these”, so routes across chains either come out wrong or take a long search. This paper gives routing that structure.

Chains as buildings, levels as floors

Each chain is a building. Its floors are levels of abstraction: base tokens at the bottom, liquidity shares above them, derivatives above those. Bridges connect the buildings. A search stays on one floor before it moves between floors or chains, which keeps the part of the graph it has to touch small.

AND and OR

Routing then becomes a search over a weighted AND/OR graph. An OR node can be reached through any one of its predecessors, so it behaves like an ordinary shortest-path step. An AND node needs all of its predecessors at once, so its cost adds up every incoming edge. Algorithm 1 in the paper, Bounded-AND Exact Path Enumeration, updates the cost of a node v reached from u exactly this way:

OR C′(v) ← C(u) + w(u, v) AND C′(v) ← C(u) + Σx ∈ Γ−(v) w(x, v) Γ−(v) is the set of predecessors of v. The update runs only while the AND depth stays within d′ ≤ 2.

Why depth 2

In general, finding the cheapest route on an AND/OR graph is NP-hard; the paper shows it with a reduction from 3-SAT. Real assets rarely nest deeper than two levels (base tokens, then pool shares, then vault or derivative tokens), so the paper bounds the AND depth at 2. Under that bound, exact path enumeration is polynomial. For large graphs, a greedy hybrid of Dijkstra's algorithm that rebuilds sub-paths at each AND node runs in O(|E| log |V|).
